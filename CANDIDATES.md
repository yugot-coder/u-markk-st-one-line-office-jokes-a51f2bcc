# One-Line Office Jokes — Candidates

Competition: u-markk-st-one-line-office-jokes-a51f2bcc
Gate: /output/entry.txt, UTF-8, 1–199 characters, one line, not only whitespace.
Judge (most important first): single-line office joke; real office detail; laugh lands
in the last few words; clear on one reading; fresh wording.

## Candidates

1. Our printer only jams on documents marked urgent, which I now believe is its actual job description.
2. I put "per my last email" in my out-of-office reply so the argument can carry on without me.
3. The office coffee machine has two settings: brown water, and a noise like it's filing a formal complaint.
4. HR told us to bring our whole selves to work, so I brought the version of me that naps under the desk.
5. My calendar is so full that the only free slot is titled "cry (tentative)" — and even that has a waitlist.
6. The reply-all chain for Dave's birthday card has outlived the birthday, the cake, and two of the signatories' jobs.
7. I asked IT for a second monitor and they gave me a second ticket number instead.
8. Our trust-fall exercise ended with me on the floor, uncaught, and somehow that improved team morale.
9. The Wi-Fi password is taped to the wall, but the network is called "FBI Surveillance Van" and nobody will admit to it.
10. My boss said his door is always open, so I walked in — and he was on a call, with the door.

## Picked winner

**#1 — "Our printer only jams on documents marked urgent, which I now believe is its actual job description."**

Why it is the best against the judge's criteria:

- **Real office detail:** the printer jamming at the worst moment is the most universally
  lived office frustration, and "marked urgent" makes it concrete, not generic work talk.
- **Punchline on the final beat:** the whole joke turns on the last three words —
  "its actual job description" — reframing the malfunction as the machine's purpose.
- **Clear on one reading:** no setup to explain, no wordplay that needs a second pass.
- **Fresh wording:** it does not recycle the tired "this meeting could have been an email"
  line or any other internet-going-round office joke; the twist (the jam *is* the job)
  is its own.
- **Economy:** 100 characters, every word earning its place, nothing left to trim.

Runners-up: #6 (reply-all birthday card) has a great final beat but runs longer and needs
the reader to track three items; #7 (second ticket number) is crisp but the punchline is
a single swap rather than a reframing. #1 wins on the twist landing hardest in the last
few words.
